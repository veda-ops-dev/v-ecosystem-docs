# V Forge Content Execution Module

## Purpose

This document defines the bounded capability area within V Forge for
content-driven owned-surface execution work.

It exists to answer:

```text
What is the V Forge content execution module, what does it own within V Forge,
what may it consume and from whom, what must it never do, and how do content
graph operations, publication continuity, execution intelligence, bounded
execution-side research, and return-to-planning posture work within this
capability area?
```

This is a Tier 2 V Forge capability authority document.

---

## Scope

This document governs:

- the identity and bounded ownership of the content execution capability area
  within V Forge
- what content-driven execution work is admitted into this capability area
- how content graph operations are conducted and constrained here
- how bounded execution-side research is admitted and its boundary with
  planning-side discovery
- how execution intelligence operates within this capability area
- the publication posture for owned content surfaces
- how SEO Machine-pattern commands and agents are classified at doctrine level
- the return-to-planning posture for content execution findings
- the relationships between this capability area and Project V, VEDA, and
  VEDA Strategy

---

## Core Rule

The content execution module is a bounded capability area within V Forge.

It exists to execute approved content-driven owned-surface work — building,
updating, publishing, and maintaining content assets on owned execution
surfaces — and to preserve execution truth and content graph truth for that work.

This capability area follows all V Forge invariants without modification or
relaxation. Content work does not make V Forge a planner, a signal system,
or a content strategy engine.

Execution of content work begins from a valid governed handoff. It ends at a
classified outcome. All research activity inside this capability area is bounded
to what approved execution requires. Publication is governance-sensitive.
Planning-relevant findings return upstream through the governed path.

---

## Module Identity

### What this capability area is

The content execution module is the V Forge capability area for executing
approved content-driven owned-surface work.

The domain it applies to includes:

- content-driven internet-marketing execution
- owned website and content surface production
- content graph maintenance for built projects
- internal linking, topic, entity, and cluster work as executed and built truth
- publication and update flows for owned content surfaces
- ranking-oriented content execution against admitted execution scope

### What this capability area is not

This capability area is not all of V Forge. V Forge supports execution work
across multiple project types. This document governs only the content-driven
owned-surface execution domain.

This capability area is not:

- a planning system
- a signal or observability system
- a content strategy engine
- a keyword research system
- an affiliate-only subsystem
- permission to turn V Forge into any of the above

The content execution module is a way of doing V Forge execution work for a
specific class of project. It does not modify V Forge's identity or boundaries.

---

## Ownership Within V Forge

Within the V Forge ownership model, this capability area owns:

- **execution truth** for content assets actually built, updated, or published
  on owned content surfaces as part of approved execution work
- **content graph truth** for what was actually built — pages, topics, entities,
  internal links, archetypes, and schema usage for content-type projects
- **publication continuity** for owned content publication work — the structured
  record of what was published, when, and in what state
- **bounded execution findings** produced during approved content work
- **execution intelligence** derived from crossing the built content graph against
  bounded VEDA signal — the analysis of how built content compares to what
  observed signal indicates
- **repeatable execution playbooks** for content creation, content update,
  content maintenance, and publication workflows within this capability area

### Ownership does not include

This capability area does not own and must not act as though it owns:

- decisions about what content should exist next — that belongs to Project V
- keyword research as a planning-side discovery activity — that belongs upstream
- raw competitive signal and evidence — that belongs to VEDA
- competitive analysis as derived strategic intelligence — that belongs to VEDA Strategy
- content opportunity scoring and gap detection as strategic intelligence —
  that belongs to VEDA Strategy
- raw signal from DataForSEO, Google Search Console, GA4, or equivalent
  providers — that belongs to VEDA
- planning cluster architecture and topic strategy — that belongs to Project V
  with appropriate strategic inputs
- provenance deception — removing AI authorship markers, scrubbing watermarks,
  or misrepresenting the origin of execution outputs is not a V Forge function
  and is not admitted in this capability area

## Execution Entry Posture

### Governed handoff required

Content execution work inside this capability area begins from a valid governed
handoff delivered through the `project-v-to-v-forge-handoff-interface.md`.

The handoff must carry at minimum:

- a stable handoff identity
- an admitted content execution scope — what asset or assets are being built
  or updated, on which surface, and under what constraints
- the planning basis that justifies the handoff
- the readiness determination that makes execution appropriate now
- conditions that should trigger return-to-planning

Before any content execution work begins, this capability area must confirm
handoff sufficiency: the admitted scope must be specific enough for V Forge to
determine what is in scope, what is out of scope, and what should trigger
clarification, escalation, or return.

### Free-form topic prompts are not a valid entry posture

An operator or LLM providing a topic, a keyword, or a general content idea
without a valid governed handoff is not an admitted execution entry point for
this capability area.

Execution does not begin from convenience. It begins from admitted scope.

If the execution scope cannot be confirmed from the handoff, V Forge must seek
governed clarification or return the gap to planning rather than proceeding on
interpretation.

## Bounded Execution-Side Research

This section governs what research activity is admitted inside this capability
area and what is not.

### The admission test

Execution-side research is admitted in this capability area only when it is
required to:

- confirm what currently exists in execution truth before beginning approved
  creation or update work
- validate that the admitted asset or scope does not already exist or overlap
  with existing built truth
- inspect existing content graph context around the admitted execution target
- determine whether the handed-off work can be completed safely and legitimately
  given current execution-side conditions
- validate bounded prerequisites before publication

### What is not admitted as execution-side research

The following are not admitted as execution-side research inside this capability
area:

- open-ended keyword research to discover what content should be built
- competitive SERP analysis to identify content opportunities
- content gap detection to find what topics are not yet covered
- content opportunity scoring to prioritize what to build next
- broad signal gathering to inform future planning direction
- market research conducted under the banner of execution

The test is direct: if the research identifies what should be built next rather
than validates whether what was already approved can be built now, it is not
execution-side research. It belongs upstream — in Project V, VEDA, or VEDA
Strategy — and must not be conducted inside V Forge execution.

### Why this boundary matters in this capability area

SEO Machine-pattern workflows commonly begin with an open research phase that
discovers and evaluates content targets. In the V Ecosystem, that discovery
phase belongs to Project V and VEDA Strategy, not to V Forge. By the time work
enters this capability area, the content target has already been approved.

V Forge does not decide what content should exist. V Forge executes content
that has been decided.

## Content Graph Operations

### Content graph truth follows execution

Content graph records in this capability area represent what was actually built.
They are registered after execution creates or materially changes an asset, not
before.

The content graph must not be used to:

- pre-model assets that are only planned
- encode future desired structure as though it already exists
- model hypothetical cluster architecture before execution produces it
- serve as a shadow planning record

### Project scope integrity

All content graph records in this capability area are project-scoped. No content
graph record may connect assets, topics, entities, or internal links across
project boundaries.

Cross-project content graph contamination is a structural failure per
`system-invariants.md` invariant 8.

### Graph registration timing

A content graph record should be registered at the earliest point where the
relevant execution truth is real enough to be durable — meaning the asset or
structural change actually exists as a built result, not merely as a draft or
a proposed state.

Partial or failed execution must be reflected honestly. A graph that overstates
what was built is worse than a graph that is incomplete.

### Graph updates follow content changes

When execution materially changes an asset — its structure, its internal links,
its topic relationships, its archetype, or its schema usage — the content graph
must be updated to reflect that change. A stale graph that no longer represents
built execution truth is an execution continuity failure.

Content graph operations in this capability area are governed by
`content-graph-operations.md`. This section states the content-execution-specific
application of those rules, not a replacement for them.

## Execution Intelligence

### What execution intelligence means here

Execution intelligence in this capability area is the bounded analytical layer
produced by crossing the built content graph against bounded VEDA signal.

Examples of questions this produces answers for:

- does built content coverage match the keyword signal relevant to the admitted
  execution scope?
- are there structural gaps in the content graph relative to VEDA-observed
  topic signal?
- where is internal link structure weak relative to what was built?
- how is a published asset performing relative to the signal VEDA observes for
  its topic area?

### Bounded analytical tools

Analytical and scoring tools — readability scorers, keyword density analyzers,
content quality raters, content length comparators, or equivalent tools — are
admissible as bounded execution-time tools within this capability area when
they operate on already-built content graph truth and bounded VEDA signal
inputs delivered through the governed interface.

These tools are bounded aids to execution. Their outputs are execution
intelligence. They are treated at doctrine level as bounded execution-time
analytical tools or plugins. The specific implementation mechanism is not
settled in this document.

### Execution intelligence does not authorize scope expansion

An execution intelligence output — a gap detected, a low score produced, a
coverage shortfall identified — is a bounded analytical result. It is not
authorization to create additional content, expand the execution scope, or
initiate new work.

If an execution intelligence output reveals a gap that would require work
beyond the admitted scope to address, that gap is a bounded finding. If the
finding has planning-level significance — meaning it would materially change
what Project V should plan next — it must return to planning through the
governed return-to-planning interface.

Execution intelligence may justify:

- a maintenance candidate within admitted scope
- a bounded correction to already-built content
- a return-to-planning finding where the gap implies planning reconsideration

It must not justify:

- silent expansion of the content execution scope
- self-authorized creation of additional assets
- strategic decisions about what content should exist

### VEDA signal consumption

Signal consumed from VEDA through the governed startup delivery or active query
interface remains VEDA-owned. V Forge may preserve bounded references to signal
used during execution intelligence analysis. V Forge must not accumulate VEDA
signal into a local archive that duplicates VEDA's observatory function.

Consuming signal is not owning signal.

## VEDA Strategy Signal Posture

VEDA Strategy is the derived strategic intelligence system of the V Ecosystem.
It derives bounded strategic intelligence from VEDA observatory records —
including strategic-level gap signals, competitive analysis conclusions, and
opportunity signals. Outputs from VEDA Strategy may be relevant to execution
intelligence operations in this capability area.

The governed interface for VEDA Strategy to V Forge signal delivery is defined
in `../interfaces/veda-strategy-to-v-forge-signal-interface.md`. That document
is a governed stub; full delivery mechanics, package field specifications, and
activity trail requirements are deferred to Batch K.

The following rules from that interface are settled and apply here:

- VEDA Strategy initiates delivery of bounded strategic signal packages to
  V Forge through the governed interface — V Forge does not query VEDA Strategy
  directly on demand
- VEDA Strategy owns the strategic signal package and the derived records it
  contains; delivery to V Forge does not transfer that ownership
- V Forge's use of a delivered strategic signal package is an execution-side
  reference — it informs execution intelligence but does not replace it
- the delivered package does not carry raw VEDA observatory records — those
  remain in VEDA's canonical ownership
- the delivered package does not constitute an execution command, a scope
  change authorization, or a planning directive
- changes to execution scope require the governed planning path through
  Project V, not a strategic signal from VEDA Strategy

This capability area must not accumulate VEDA Strategy intelligence
independently or treat delivered signal packages as canonical V Forge truth.
V Forge's execution intelligence derived from crossing its content graph against
VEDA Strategy signal inputs remains V Forge execution truth — the underlying
VEDA Strategy derived records remain in `veda_strategy.*`.

## Publication Posture

### Publication is governance-sensitive

Publishing content to an owned surface — making a page publicly accessible or
updating a live page — is a governance-sensitive action.

In the approval model defined in `../governance/approval-and-escalation-model.md`,
publication to a live owned surface is a **Class C** action. It is
external-facing, public-consequence, and in most cases irreversible in the sense
that once content is indexed and accessible, the publication has occurred.

Class C actions require explicit governed approval before they become active.
Informal operator direction, conversational confirmation, or slash command
invocation alone does not satisfy Class C approval posture.

### CMS draft state vs live publication

Creating a content asset as a draft on a CMS — never published, not publicly
accessible — is different in consequence from live publication.

The precise approval class for draft creation (Class B vs Class C) is not fully
settled in current repo doctrine. This document takes the conservative position:

- Live publication of content to an owned surface is Class C and requires
  explicit governed approval.
- CMS draft creation where content will not be publicly accessible may be
  Class B, but this must be confirmed against the approval and escalation model
  when the seam is settled. Until then, treat draft creation as requiring
  operator confirmation and do not assume it is approval-free.

### Pre-publication verification

Before publication of a content asset, this capability area must confirm:

- the admitted execution scope for this asset is complete
- the content graph record for this asset is registered or updated
- no mid-execution findings exist that would block legitimate publication
- authorization posture for the publication action is satisfied

Pre-publication verification is an input to the publication approval. It is
not the approval itself.

### Publication continuity

When a publication action occurs, a publication continuity record must be written.
This record is part of V Forge execution truth for this capability area.

Publication continuity must be updated atomically with the publication event.
A publication event without a continuity record is an execution continuity gap.

### Failure honesty

Partial publication, failed publication, and rollback are valid outcome states.
They must be reported honestly. A publication that fails or produces only partial
results must not be reported as complete. Uncertainty about publication outcome
must be preserved, not smoothed over.

## Classification of SEO Machine-Pattern Tools

This section classifies SEO Machine-pattern execution tools at doctrine level.
This classification governs whether and how those patterns are admitted inside
this capability area.

### Admitted with adaptation

**Content creation commands (write-pattern)** — commands that produce a new
content asset — are admitted as execution playbook commands only when:
- the content target is already admitted via a valid governed handoff
- the command confirms handoff sufficiency before proceeding
- scope bounds are checked during execution
- execution continuity records are maintained
- publication requires governed authorization before any publication action

**Content update commands (update/rewrite/optimize-pattern)** — commands that
modify an existing content asset — are admitted as execution playbook commands
only under the same conditions as creation commands, applied to bounded update
scope.

**Analysis commands (analyze-existing-pattern)** — commands that inspect an
existing content asset and produce a bounded analytical output — are admitted
as execution intelligence tools when they remain within project scope and produce
execution-side observations, not planning-level decisions.

**Specialist agents (SEO optimizer, internal linker, keyword mapper, content
analyzer, editor-pattern)** — bounded analytical agents that fire after a
primary execution step — are admitted as bounded specialist aids. Each agent
must:
- operate within the project and execution scope of the parent session
- produce a bounded typed output
- not self-authorize scope expansion based on its output
- be operator-visible as a delegated step, not silently chained

### Not admitted

**Research commands (`/research`-pattern)** — commands that conduct open-ended
keyword research, SERP analysis, competitor gap analysis, or content opportunity
discovery — are **not admitted** inside this capability area.

This is a firm classification, not a grey area. Open-ended research to discover
what should be built belongs in Project V and VEDA Strategy. By the time work
enters this capability area, the research phase is over and the content scope is
admitted. A command that begins from a free-form topic and discovers what to
build is conducting planning-side work inside an execution module. It must not
be adapted into this capability area without fundamental restructuring that would
require it to begin from admitted execution scope.

**Provenance deception commands (`/scrub`-pattern)** — commands that remove
AI authorship markers, scrub watermarks, or otherwise misrepresent the provenance
of execution outputs — are **not admitted** in this capability area.

V Forge execution outputs are what they are. Honest execution truth includes
honest provenance. Misrepresenting the origin of content produced during
execution conflicts with V Forge's reporting doctrine and the boundary-safe
reporting invariant.

**Automatic cascade execution without operator visibility** — the pattern of
automatically chaining multiple agents after an execution step without operator
visibility and without scope confirmation at each step — is not admitted.
Each delegated execution step must be visible. Scope confirmation must occur
before work proceeds beyond the admitted unit.

## Return-to-Planning Posture

### When to return to planning

This capability area must use the governed return-to-planning interface when:

- the admitted content scope no longer makes sense under current execution
  conditions — for example, the asset conflicts with something already built
  that was not reflected in the handoff
- the real content need is materially larger than the admitted execution unit
  and proceeding would amount to silent scope expansion
- the content asset reveals a planning-level contradiction — for example,
  the topic structure does not match the cluster architecture that planning intended
- publication cannot proceed legitimately inside current execution authority
- execution intelligence reveals a gap or mismatch with planning-level significance
  — meaning it would materially change what Project V should plan next, not
  merely what maintenance should fix locally
- execution fails or blocks in a way that requires planning reconsideration
  rather than local recovery

### When to fix locally

This capability area may fix locally, without returning to planning, when:

- the fix is a bounded correction to already-built content within admitted scope
  — for example, correcting a broken internal link or updating schema markup
- the graph issue is an execution-truth error, not a planning-level contradiction
- the fix does not widen execution scope
- the fix does not change what the planning basis assumed about the content target

### The boundary test

If the finding changes what Project V should plan to do next — what content
should exist, what structure should be built, what the topic architecture should
look like — it must return to planning.

If the finding can be resolved as a correction to what was built within the
admitted scope — a graph repair, a content correction, a structural fix — it
may be handled locally.

When the boundary is unclear, the conservative default is to surface the finding
and seek operator guidance rather than proceeding on interpretation.

### Return package posture

Return-to-planning packages from this capability area must preserve:

- what content execution work was completed or attempted
- what the finding is and why it requires planning reconsideration
- what V Forge continues to own after the return
- what execution truth state exists at the time of return

Return packages must not dump all execution state upstream. They transfer
findings. V Forge retains execution history.

The return interface is governed by:
`../interfaces/v-forge-to-project-v-return-to-planning-interface.md`

## Relationship to Project V

Project V hands off approved content work to this capability area through the
governed handoff interface.

This capability area executes that work and may return bounded execution findings
back to Project V when planning reconsideration is required.

Project V remains the planning and orchestration system of record.
This capability area remains the execution system of record for content work.

This capability area must not:

- decide what content topics should be pursued
- create new planning structure for content work
- reinterpret cluster or topic architecture without the return-to-planning path
- use content execution findings to self-authorize scope expansion

---

## Relationship to VEDA

VEDA delivers bounded startup signal to this capability area at the initialization
of an active content execution scope, through the governed signal interface.

This capability area may request bounded mid-execution evidence through the
governed evidence access interface where execution requires it.

VEDA remains the canonical owner of all signal and evidence it delivers.
This capability area uses signal. It does not own signal. It does not accumulate
signal archives. It does not conduct open-ended observatory research.

If this capability area needs signal that VEDA is not providing, the correct
path is to surface that need through the governed operator path — not to build
independent signal ingestion.

---

## Relationship to VEDA Strategy

VEDA Strategy is the derived strategic intelligence system that reads VEDA
observatory truth and produces bounded strategic signals including content
opportunity scoring, gap detection, and competitive positioning.

These signals are relevant upstream — to the planning basis that Project V
uses when deciding what content execution to hand off. They may appear as
context in a handoff package.

The direct signal interface between VEDA Strategy and V Forge is not yet
defined as a governed doc. Until it is, this capability area must not assume
or invent a direct query path to VEDA Strategy.

## Anti-Drift Rules

### 1. No shadow planning rule
Content execution findings and execution intelligence outputs do not authorize
new content scope. Discovering a gap is not permission to fill it.

### 2. No shadow observatory rule
This capability area must not source signal from external providers directly.
Signal comes through VEDA. Analytical tools operate on what was built and on
bounded VEDA signal. They do not become independent research engines.

### 3. No execution-is-strategy rule
High execution velocity and useful analytical output do not make this capability
area a strategy system. Producing good content efficiently does not authorize
expanding what content is produced.

### 4. No provenance deception rule
Execution outputs are honestly represented. Content produced in this capability
area is execution output. Its provenance is not obscured.

### 5. No cascade-without-visibility rule
Specialist agents and analytical tools must be visible to the operator as
delegated execution steps. Silent automatic chaining without operator visibility
is not admitted.

### 6. No draft-is-free rule
Draft creation on a CMS is not treated as a free Class A action. It is at
minimum Class B and should be handled conservatively until the approval seam
is settled.

### 7. No research-as-execution rule
Open-ended research to discover what should be built is not admitted as
bounded execution-side research. The `/research`-pattern command is not
admitted in its current form.

## Human-In-The-Loop Principle

Human review remains especially important in this capability area when:

- publication actions reach the Class C gate
- execution intelligence produces findings that may require planning
  reconsideration
- content scope ambiguity emerges mid-execution
- partial or failed publication has occurred
- the execution target differs meaningfully from what the handoff described

This capability area should make content execution more reliable and legible.
It must not make governance disappear behind execution speed.

---

## LLM Use Principle

A capable LLM operating inside this capability area should understand that:

- content execution begins from admitted execution scope, not from topics or
  keyword ideas
- the content graph reflects what was built, not what might be built
- execution intelligence is bounded analytical output — it is not permission to
  expand scope
- VEDA signal is consumed, not owned
- publication is governance-sensitive — draft or live
- the `/research`-pattern is not admitted here
- findings with planning-level significance return to planning, not to a local
  content queue

If an LLM uses this capability area to conduct open-ended content research,
absorb signal ownership, expand content scope without a governing handoff, or
publish content without a Class C approval gate, this document is failing.

## Usage

This document should be used:

- when determining what content execution work is admitted inside V Forge
- when reviewing whether a proposed content execution command or agent is
